The descriptions below follow the same voice and framework. Throughout, the five
interfaces of System 5 are the **Host(1)**, **Conscious Knowledge(2)**, **Emotional
Knowledge(3)**, **Routines(4)** and **Behavioral Form(5)**, stacked hierarchically with
interface (1) inside (2) inside (3) inside (4) inside (5), per the Primary Universal
Hierarchy (see `T-9 Primary Universal Term` and pages 68–69 of
[`Sys 5 - Dan Explorations - v7`](<extracted/sys-5-dan-explorations-v7/sys-5-dan-explorations-v7.md>)).
Each Term has an **expressive mode** and a **regenerative mode** in which interfaces (1)
and (2) change places across the perceptual axis. The **Subjectively Oriented Autonomic
Terms (T1-1 … T1-9)** work through the autonomic nervous system via the limbic system;
the **Objectively Oriented Somatic Terms (T1 … T9)** work through the somatic nervous
system via the sensory and motor cortices; the **Transjectively Oriented Universal Terms
(T2-5, T2-7)** span both series, transferring discretion and goal between them.

## Subjectively Oriented Autonomic Terms

### T1-4 — Organized Input (T4S)
*Proprioceptive Registration of Enacted Routine*

The virtual urge assessed in T1-2 and restrained into a coherent virtual image in T1-3
is now registered as organized input. Routines(4) coalesce with Behavioral Form(5) so
that the chemical and postural resources of the body report back through the autonomic
afferents to Emotional Knowledge(3). The Host(1), coalesced with Conscious Knowledge(2),
subjectively Knows the Emotional(3) registration of Routines(4) as they are organized
within the Form(5) of the body. The muscle spindles primed by the gamma motor simulation
of T1-1 now return proprioceptive input, so the felt need is confirmed, corrected or
denied by the actual disposition of the body. In the regenerative mode (1) and (2)
change places: the Host(1) aligns with Emotions(3) as they relate objectively to the
organized Routines(4) held in Consciousness(2), consolidating the input as a settled
emotional context for action. The person(1) knows(2) that the felt urge(3) is organized
in the body's routines(4) as they actually stand in physical circumstance(5). This does
not yet commit the body to act; it organizes the input on which action can proceed.

### T1-5 — Physical Action (T5S)
*Sympathetic Commitment of the Body*

The organized input of T1-4 releases into physical action. The Host(1), coalesced with
Conscious Knowledge(2), Emotionally(3) commits Routines(4) that animate the Behavioral
Form(5) of the body. The sympathetic nervous system mobilizes the chemical resources
assessed in T1-2 — heart rate, respiration and visceral tone are recruited in support of
the behavioral routine, while the parasympathetic restraint of T1-3 is relaxed only to
the degree the virtual image allows. The action is subjectively fueled: it is the felt
urge finding expression through the body rather than the body being steered from
outside. In the regenerative mode (1) and (2) change places so that the Host(1) is
aligned with limbic Emotions(3) as they relate objectively to the Routines(4) being
spent, and Consciousness(2) monitors the expenditure against the virtual image. The
person(1) knows(2) an emotional commitment(3) spending itself through patterned
routines(4) into physical behavior(5). The action remains answerable to the perceived
need of T1-1: energy is committed only as far as the assessed capacity of T1-2 permits.

### T1-6 — Corporeal Body (T6S)
*The Felt Body as Lived Form*

With action underway the emphasis falls on the Corporeal Body itself. Behavioral
Form(5) coalesces with Routines(4) as one lived body within which Emotional Knowledge(3)
is immersed. The Host(1), coalesced with Conscious Knowledge(2), subjectively inhabits
this corporeal coalescence: the body is not perceived as an object among objects but is
felt from within as the seat of the urge that acts. Visceral, hormonal and postural
states are integrated by the limbic system into a single felt sense of the body in its
circumstance. In the regenerative mode (1) and (2) change places: the Host(1) aligned
with Emotions(3) objectively registers the body's Routines(4) and Form(5) as they have
been altered by the action of T1-5, and Consciousness(2) receives this alteration as a
change in the felt body. The person(1) knows(2) the emotional state(3) of their own
embodied routines(4) as one corporeal form(5). This felt body is the standing condition
against which the following Terms measure, remember and balance what the action has
wrought.

### T1-7 — Quantized Memory (T7S)
*Emotional Consolidation of the Act*

The lived episode of T1-5 and T1-6 is now quantized as memory. Routines(4) and
Behavioral Form(5) close face to face and coalesce as one element of technique that
belongs to Emotional Knowledge(3) — a quantized element recalled to the Void, timeless
and formless, as in Particular Term 3 of System 3. The Host(1), coalesced with Conscious
Knowledge(2), subjectively Knows this emotional consolidation: the felt success or
failure of the act is bound into the limbic pattern from which future urges will be
recalled. In the regenerative mode (1) and (2) change places so that the Host(1),
aligned with Emotions(3), objectively deposits the consolidated Routine(4) in relation
to Consciousness(2); the memory is thereby available for recall in a future T1-1 field
of perception. The person(1) knows(2) that the feeling(3) of the completed routine(4)
has been stored as a discrete element of embodied technique(5). Each such quantum is
holistically integrated with all others; nothing of the emotional history of the person
is lost, though not all of it is consciously accessible.

### T1-8 — Balanced Response (T8S)
*Homeostatic Reconciliation of Urge and Outcome*

The consolidated memory of T1-7 is weighed against the original perception of need of
T1-1. The bipolar balance between R1 and R2 that generated the virtual image in T1-2 is
now checked in fact: the Host(1), coalesced with Conscious Knowledge(2), Emotionally(3)
compares the Routines(4) as enacted with the Behavioral Form(5) as altered, and the
autonomic nervous system restores homeostasis accordingly. Sympathetic expenditure is
reconciled with parasympathetic restraint; visceral tone, chemical resources and
emotional charge are returned toward equilibrium, discharging the urge to the degree the
need was met and re-arming it to the degree it was not. In the regenerative mode (1) and
(2) change places: the Host(1) aligned with Emotions(3) objectively accepts the balance
struck in Routines(4) as it is registered by Consciousness(2). The person(1) knows(2)
whether the felt need(3) has been satisfied by the patterned response(4) in the
circumstances of the world(5). An unbalanced residue is carried forward as a renewed
perception of need in the next cycle.

### T1-9 — Universal Discretion (T9S)
*Subjective Universal Hierarchy*

This is the subjective counterpart of the Primary Universal Term. The five interfaces
are stacked as one discretionary hierarchy felt from within: the Host Idea(1) directs
Conscious Knowledge(2) which directs Emotional Knowledge(3) which directs autonomic
Routines(4) which direct the Behavioral Form(5) of the body. Being primary to the recall
process on the subjective side, it has implicit discretionary characteristics: it
selects, from the quantized emotional memory of T1-7 and the balance struck in T1-8,
which urges are admitted into the next field of perception (T1-1). It is associated with
the archetype of the person — the Secondary Universal Term working in conjunction with
the Primary Universal Term of the species — and it prescribes the five successive Steps
of each System 5 Cycle as they are lived subjectively. In the regenerative mode the
hierarchy is traversed in return: Form(5) informs Routines(4) which inform Emotional
Knowledge(3) which informs Consciousness(2) in relation to the Host(1). The person(1)
consciously(2) exercises discretion over which emotional patterns(3) may fuel
routines(4) that commit the body(5). It is felt as conscience: the quiet authority by
which some urges are owned and others declined.

## Objectively Oriented Somatic Terms

### T1 — Need Perception (T1O)
*The Field of Perception as Physical Circumstance*

The objective counterpart of T1-1. The Host(1) person perceives the physical
circumstance of the world through the somatic senses. The Host(1) is coalesced with
Conscious Knowledge(2) which is objectively aligned with Behavioral Form(5) as it is
coalesced with Routines(4) via the sensory cortex of the new brain. The Host(1) thus
Knows(2) the Formed(5) Routines(4) of the outside world — objects, persons and events as
patterned appearances — the perception being subjectively referred back to Emotional
Knowledge(3) for its felt significance. Exteroceptive input from eyes, ears and skin is
constructed by the nervous system into a virtual mirror of the outside world from the
unique perspective of the person. In the regenerative mode (1) and (2) change places:
the Host(1) stands on the objective side of the axis, aligned with the world's
Forms(5) as they relate back through Routines(4) to Consciousness(2), scanning the scene
for what answers to need. The person(1) knows(2) a patterned circumstance(4,5) that
occasions a need felt within(3). This does not itself interpret the circumstance; it
delivers the objective field within which need can be recognized.

### T2 — Idea Creation (T2O)
*Recall of a Responsive Idea from the Void*

Within the perceived field of T1 an idea is created — or more precisely recalled from
the Void — that answers the circumstance. The Host(1), coalesced with Conscious
Knowledge(2), accesses the quantized elements of memory in which Routines(4) and
Form(5) are eternally married as technique, and lifts one into consciousness as a
possible response. The idea is objectively oriented: it is a candidate shape of action
in the world, not yet an urge of the body. Emotional Knowledge(3) attends it as
interest, coloring the recalled idea with felt relevance. In the regenerative mode (1)
and (2) change places so that Consciousness(2) holds the idea against the Host's(1)
situation, testing it for fit against the perceived Forms(5) and available Routines(4).
The person(1) consciously(2) conceives a patterned response(4) to the forms of
circumstance(5), informed by its felt significance(3). Many ideas may be created and
dismissed within a single cycle; only an idea that survives the fit is carried forward
to transfer.

### T3 — Idea Transfer (T3O)
*Commitment of the Idea to the Nervous Infrastructure*

The idea created in T2 is transferred from Conscious Knowledge(2) into the executive
infrastructure of the nervous system. The Host(1) directs Consciousness(2) to commit the
idea downward through Emotional Knowledge(3) into premotor Routines(4): the abstract
shape of the response is translated into a sequenced program of neuro-muscular activity
addressed to the Form(5) of the body and its instruments. This is the objective analogue
of the virtual image triad of T1-3 — where the subjective series restrains the urge, the
objective series programs the act. The transfer is hierarchical and consistent with the
Primary Universal Term: (1) inside (2) inside (3) inside (4) inside (5). In the
regenerative mode (1) and (2) change places, and the drafted program is read back to
Consciousness(2) for confirmation against the idea before release. The person(1)
consciously(2) hands a formed intention through its felt sanction(3) to the routines(4)
that will body it forth(5). Once transferred, the idea no longer needs conscious
rehearsal; it stands ready as organized input to action.

### T4 — Organized Input (T4O)
*Sensory Organization of the Field of Action*

Before and during the act, the somatic senses organize the input that the programmed
Routines(4) require. Behavioral Form(5) — the disposition of the body and of the
relevant objects of the world — is registered through exteroceptive and proprioceptive
channels and organized by the sensory cortex in the service of the transferred idea.
The Host(1), coalesced with Conscious Knowledge(2), objectively Knows the world's
Forms(5) as organized within the frame of the intended Routines(4): distances, weights,
positions and timings are given as parameters of action. Emotional Knowledge(3) weighs
the organized input for risk and opportunity. In the regenerative mode (1) and (2)
change places so that the organized input is checked against the idea held in
Consciousness(2), and discrepancies re-enter T2 as occasions for revised ideas. The
person(1) knows(2) the field of action(5) as structured input for the patterned
response(4), felt(3) as readiness. The muscle spindles primed in the subjective series
(T1-1) here deliver their proprioceptive report, closing the loop between the autonomic
and somatic orientations.

### T5 — Physical Action (T5O)
*Motor Execution in the World*

The programmed Routines(4) are executed through the motor cortex and the somatic
efferents, altering the Behavioral Form(5) of the body in concert with the whole
physical universe. The Host(1), coalesced with Conscious Knowledge(2), directs the act;
Emotional Knowledge(3) fuels it with the commitment struck in the subjective series
(T1-5); Routines(4) sequence it; Form(5) realizes it. The action is objectively
oriented: its measure is the change it works in the world, not the discharge of the
urge. Feedback is continuous — each phase of the movement returns proprioceptive and
exteroceptive input (T4) that steers the next phase, the counter-current balance of R1
and R2 operating across the Routine(4) interface. In the regenerative mode (1) and (2)
change places: the Host(1) stands with the world's response while Consciousness(2)
compares the unfolding Form(5) with the transferred idea (T3), correcting the
Routines(4) in flight. The person(1) consciously(2) enacts a patterned routine(4) that
changes the form of circumstance(5), sustained by felt commitment(3).

### T6 — Corporeal Body (T6O)
*The Body as Object among Objects*

In the objective series the Corporeal Body appears as a thing in the world — a Form(5)
among forms, bounded, located and subject to physical law. Routines(4) and Form(5) are
mutually closed as the particular thing that the person's body is, intimately bound as
One yet spatially separate from other bodies of the same kind, as every hydrogen atom is
separate though each consists of an intimately bound photon, electron and proton. The
Host(1), coalesced with Conscious Knowledge(2), objectively Knows its own body as it is
seen, weighed and handled — the same body that T1-6 knows from within as the felt seat
of urge. Emotional Knowledge(3) mediates between the two knowings. In the regenerative
mode (1) and (2) change places, and the objective body-image is reconciled with the
felt body: posture, capacity and appearance are updated in Consciousness(2) from the
outcome of action (T5). The person(1) knows(2) their body(5) with its routines(4) as a
particular thing in the world, emotionally owned(3). This objective body-image is the
reference against which memory and balance are struck in T7 and T8.

### T7 — Quantized Memory (T7O)
*Consolidation of Technique*

The executed act is consolidated as objective technique. Routines(4) and Form(5) close
face to face and coalesce as a quantized element of memory in the Void — the shape of
the act married to the shape of its result, formless and timeless, holistically
integrated with all such elements as in Particular Term 3 of System 3. The Host(1),
coalesced with Conscious Knowledge(2), Knows the consolidation as acquired skill: what
worked is bound into the repertoire from which T2 will recall future ideas. Emotional
Knowledge(3) tags the quantum with its felt worth, joining it to the emotional
consolidation of T1-7. In the regenerative mode (1) and (2) change places so that the
stored technique is registered to the Host(1) as a settled capacity rather than an
event remembered. The person(1) knows(2) that the routine(4) and its formed result(5)
are stored as one discrete element of technique, felt(3) as competence. The repertoire
of such quanta constitutes the person's practical knowledge of the world.

### T8 — Balanced Response (T8O)
*Double-Entry Reconciliation of Intention and Result*

The consolidated technique of T7 is balanced against the transferred idea of T3 and the
perceived need of T1. The Host(1), coalesced with Conscious Knowledge(2), objectively
compares the Form(5) achieved with the Form intended, across the Routines(4) actually
spent — a double-entry bookkeeping of intention and result in which the projections
(Pk) account what was received and what was expended, while the Relational Wholes (Rn)
hold the counter-current balance of R1 and R2. Emotional Knowledge(3) contributes the
reconciled homeostatic balance from T1-8, so that the objective account and the
subjective discharge agree. In the regenerative mode (1) and (2) change places and the
residue of the account — need unmet, error uncorrected, technique unproven — is posted
back to Consciousness(2) as the opening condition of the next cycle. The person(1)
knows(2) whether the enacted response(4) balanced the perceived circumstance(5),
and feels(3) the account settled or still open. A balanced response completes the
cycle; an unbalanced one renews it.

## Transjectively Oriented Universal Terms

### T2-5 — Discretion Transfer (T05)
*Transfer of Universal Discretion between the Autonomic and Somatic Series*

The two Universal Discretion Terms — T1-9 on the subjective autonomic side and T9 on
the objective somatic side — cannot act in isolation: every System 5 Cycle requires
that the discretion exercised over felt urges be the same discretion exercised over
enacted responses. T2-5 is the transjective Term that transfers discretion between the
two hierarchies. In it the two open interfaces, Host(1) and Conscious Knowledge(2),
coalesce as one across both series while Emotional Knowledge(3), Routines(4) and
Behavioral Form(5) remain a mutually closed triad, so that the one discretionary
authority relates from within each of the three closed interfaces of either series, as
in System 2 the universal Centre 1 relates from within each particular Centre 2. It is
interjective in the manner of Term 3 of System 4: it does not itself perceive, act or
remember, but licenses which autonomic urges may recruit somatic routines and which
somatic programs may draw on autonomic fuel. In the expressive mode discretion passes
from the subjective hierarchy to sanction objective action; in the regenerative mode
discretion returns from the objective account (T8) to re-order the subjective
priorities (T1-8, T1-9). The person is one agent, not two: what conscience(1,2)
permits within(3,4,5 subjectively) is what conduct(3,4,5 objectively) may perform.
Being universal it does not exist as a thing in space and time; it is archetypal,
confined within and linking the particular Terms of both series.

### T2-7 — Goal Transfer (T07)
*Transfer of Goal between the Autonomic and Somatic Series*

Where T2-5 transfers the authority to act, T2-7 transfers the goal of action. The need
perceived and assessed subjectively (T1-1, T1-2) must become the objective aim that
idea creation and transfer (T2, T3) serve; and the result achieved objectively (T5–T8)
must return as satisfaction or renewal of the felt need. T2-7 is the transjective Term
that carries the goal across: the coalesced Host(1) and Conscious Knowledge(2) hold one
goal in common for both hierarchies, while the closed triad of Emotional Knowledge(3),
Routines(4) and Form(5) presents the goal on each side in its own register — as a felt
urge to be discharged on the autonomic side, and as a form to be realized in the world
on the somatic side. It corresponds to the Goal Term of System 3 (3T3, Idea=(Routine=
Form)) elaborated to five interfaces: the goal is a virtual coincidence of routine and
form held inside the idea, transferred whole between the two orientations. In the
expressive mode the goal passes from felt need to enacted aim; in the regenerative mode
the achieved form is transferred back as the measure of the need's satisfaction,
closing the Cycle that Universal Discretion (T2-5) opened. The person(1) knowingly(2)
pursues in the world(5) the same goal that is felt within(3), through one repertoire of
routines(4). Like all universal Terms it is unique and archetypal: many acts, one goal
structure, prescribing with T2-5 the coherence of the five successive Steps of each
System 5 Cycle.
